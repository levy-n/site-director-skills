## 2. Searching by Industry

Use these search strategies per industry vertical:

### Restaurant / Cafe / Food
- **Search terms:** "restaurant landing page", "cafe website design", "food delivery page"
- **Key patterns:** Full-bleed food photography, warm color palettes, menu sections, reservation CTAs
- **Hebrew note:** Food businesses in Israel often use warm tones (terracotta, olive, cream) --- avoid cold tech blues

### Medical Clinic / Doctor / Dental
- **Search terms:** "medical clinic website", "healthcare landing page", "dental website design"
- **Key patterns:** Clean white space, trust badges, doctor photos, appointment booking CTA
- **Hebrew note:** Medical in Israel leans clean and professional --- whites, light blues, greens. Avoid overly clinical/sterile feel

### Beauty Salon / Spa
- **Search terms:** "beauty salon website", "spa landing page", "wellness website"
- **Key patterns:** Elegant typography, lifestyle imagery, booking flow, service showcase
- **Hebrew note:** Rose gold, muted pinks, sage greens work well. Avoid generic "beauty" stock photos

### Law Firm / Legal
- **Search terms:** "law firm website", "attorney landing page", "legal services website"
- **Key patterns:** Authority design, dark/navy tones, partner bios, practice area cards
- **Hebrew note:** Israeli law firms trend more modern than US counterparts. Navy + gold works, but also consider clean modern approaches

### Real Estate
- **Search terms:** "real estate landing page", "property listing website"
- **Key patterns:** Large hero images, property cards, map integration, contact forms
- **Hebrew note:** Price formatting in NIS, area in sqm, address format differences

### Fitness / Personal Trainer
- **Search terms:** "fitness website design", "gym landing page", "personal trainer website"
- **Key patterns:** Bold typography, action photography, transformation testimonials, class schedules
- **Hebrew note:** Energetic design but not aggressive. Israeli fitness culture is more Mediterranean than American

### Education / Courses
- **Search terms:** "online course landing page", "education website", "course sales page"
- **Key patterns:** Curriculum breakdown, instructor credibility, student testimonials, enrollment CTA
- **Hebrew note:** Hebrew course pages need extra attention to text hierarchy due to RTL reading patterns

### General Small Business / Services
- **Search terms:** "small business website", "service company landing page"
- **Key patterns:** Hero with value prop, services grid, about section, testimonials, contact

## 3. How to Analyze Inspiration

For each reference found, extract and document the following:

### Color Analysis
```
Primary Color:    [hex] - used for [buttons/headers/accents]
Secondary Color:  [hex] - used for [backgrounds/cards/secondary elements]
Accent Color:     [hex] - used for [highlights/CTAs/hover states]
Background:       [hex] - main background
Text Primary:     [hex] - body text
Text Secondary:   [hex] - secondary/muted text
```

**Extraction methods:**
- Browser DevTools: inspect elements, check computed colors
- Screenshot + color picker tool
- Note the COLOR RATIOS --- how much of each color is used (usually 60/30/10 rule)

### Typography Analysis
```
Heading Font:     [name] - weight [weight], style [style]
Body Font:        [name] - weight [weight], line-height [value]
Display/Accent:   [name] - if used for special elements
Size Scale:       [list observed sizes from largest to smallest]
```

### Layout Patterns
```
Grid System:      [columns, max-width, gutters]
Section Heights:  [full viewport / auto / fixed]
Spacing Rhythm:   [tight / medium / generous]
Content Width:    [narrow centered / wide / full-bleed]
```

### Section Structure (Top to Bottom)
```
1. [Section Type] - [Brief description of content and visual treatment]
2. [Section Type] - ...
3. [Section Type] - ...
...
```

Common section types:
- Hero (full-screen / split / minimal)
- Social proof bar (logos / badges / numbers)
- Problem/pain section
- Solution/features section
- How it works (steps)
- Testimonials (cards / carousel / single quote)
- Pricing (if applicable)
- FAQ (accordion)
- CTA section (final conversion push)
- Footer (contact info / links / social)

### Interaction & Motion
```
Scroll Behavior:  [smooth / snap / parallax]
Animations:       [type and trigger points]
Hover Effects:    [buttons / cards / images]
Loading:          [transitions / skeleton / instant]
```

### Direction Template

```markdown
## Direction [N]: [Descriptive Name]
**Vibe:** [2-3 word mood description]
**Reference Sites:** [links to 2-3 inspirations]

### Color Palette
[Show 5-6 colors with hex codes]

### Typography Direction
- Headlines: [font suggestion]
- Body: [font suggestion]
- Character: [formal / friendly / bold / elegant]

### Layout Style
- [Description: e.g., "Clean with generous whitespace, centered content, card-based sections"]

### Key Visual Elements
- [e.g., "Rounded cards with subtle shadows"]
- [e.g., "Full-bleed hero with overlaid text"]
- [e.g., "Icon-based feature grid"]

### Why This Works for [Client Name]
- [Reason 1]
- [Reason 2]
```

### Example Directions for a Restaurant

**Direction 1: "Warm Mediterranean"** --- Earthy tones, handwritten accents, full-bleed food photography
**Direction 2: "Modern Minimal"** --- Clean whites, sharp typography, menu as focal point, elegant spacing
**Direction 3: "Bold & Vibrant"** --- Strong colors, large type, playful layout, street-food energy

## 5. Operator Selection via AskUserQuestion

After presenting directions, use AskUserQuestion to let the operator choose:

```
Presented 3 design directions for [Client Name]:

1. [Direction Name] - [one-line summary]
2. [Direction Name] - [one-line summary]
3. [Direction Name] - [one-line summary]

Which 2-3 references resonate most with your client's brand?
You can also mix elements: "I like the colors from #1 but the layout from #3"
```

**Important:** Allow mixing and matching. Operators often want colors from one direction with layout from another.

### Follow-up Questions (if needed)
- "Any elements you specifically do NOT want?"
- "Does the client have existing brand guidelines or colors we must use?"
- "Any competitor sites we should differentiate from?"

## 6. RTL / Hebrew Design Considerations

### Layout Mirroring Rules

**DO Mirror (RTL flip):**
- Text alignment (right-aligned by default)
- Navigation order (right to left)
- Icon placement relative to text (icons on right side of labels)
- Progress indicators / step flows (right to left)
- Breadcrumbs direction
- Back/forward arrows
- Checkmarks and bullet points (right side)
- Sidebar position (right side if applicable)

**DO NOT Mirror:**
- Images and photographs
- Logos
- Video player controls (these are universal)
- Phone number formatting
- Slashes in dates
- Music playback controls
- Graphs and charts (x-axis reads left to right universally)

### Hebrew Typography Spacing
- Hebrew text requires slightly more line-height than English (1.6-1.8 for body)
- Letter spacing should be 0 or very minimal for Hebrew (never track Hebrew text wide)
- Paragraph width: Hebrew reads comfortably at 60-70 characters per line
- Mixed Hebrew/English content: ensure numbers and English words within Hebrew flow correctly (bidirectional text handling)

### Visual Balance in RTL
- Hero sections with text on the right, image on the left (mirrored from English templates)
- CTA buttons: text-align center is safe for both directions
- Card grids: flex-wrap works the same, but reading order is right-to-left
- Asymmetric layouts flip naturally with `dir="rtl"` on the container

## 7. Anti-Generic Design Principles

### NEVER Use (These Make Every Site Look the Same)
- **Inter font** for everything (it is everywhere, choose something with personality)
- **Roboto** as a default (same problem as Inter)
- **Purple/violet gradients** (the 2020-2023 startup cliche)
- **Blue-to-purple gradients** on CTAs (overused to death)
- **Generic geometric patterns** as backgrounds
- **Unsplash stock photos** without editing/treatment (especially the handshake and laptop photos)
- **Identical card layouts** with icon + title + paragraph repeated 3 times
- **"We are passionate about..."** type copy
- **Floating abstract shapes** as decoration (circles, blobs)
- **Default Material Design shadows**

### DO Instead
- Choose fonts that reflect the client's personality, not the designer's defaults
- Use the client's actual colors or derive a unique palette from their industry
- Real photography (client's actual photos) > stock, always
- If stock is necessary: edit it, crop it, apply a brand color overlay, treat it
- Asymmetric layouts over perfectly centered grids
- Custom illustrations or icons over generic icon packs
- Textures from the real world (paper, fabric, concrete) over digital gradients
- One strong visual idea per page, not five competing effects

### The "Would I Know Whose Site This Is?" Test
After designing, ask: if you removed the logo and company name, could you tell which business this site belongs to? If not, it is too generic.

## 9. Hebrew Design Aesthetics by Industry

### Food / Restaurant
- **Colors:** Warm earth tones --- terracotta, olive, cream, burnt orange, deep brown
- **Fonts:** Rounded Hebrew fonts (Rubik, Assistant), handwritten Hebrew display fonts
- **Feel:** Warm, inviting, appetite-inducing
- **Avoid:** Cold blues, overly tech aesthetic

### Medical / Health
- **Colors:** Whites, soft blues, mint greens, light grays
- **Fonts:** Clean, highly readable (Heebo, Noto Sans Hebrew)
- **Feel:** Trustworthy, clean, professional, calming
- **Avoid:** Overly playful elements, aggressive colors

### Legal / Finance
- **Colors:** Navy, dark green, gold accents, charcoal, cream
- **Fonts:** Serious serif-like Hebrew fonts or clean sans (Heebo medium/bold)
- **Feel:** Authoritative, stable, trustworthy
- **Avoid:** Trendy design, playful animations, casual fonts

### Beauty / Wellness
- **Colors:** Rose, sage, cream, blush, muted gold, soft lavender
- **Fonts:** Elegant Hebrew fonts (Secular One for display, Assistant for body)
- **Feel:** Premium, feminine (if relevant), serene
- **Avoid:** Neon colors, aggressive typography

### Tech / Startup
- **Colors:** Unique palette derived from brand --- NOT default blue/purple gradient
- **Fonts:** Modern Hebrew (Heebo, Rubik)
- **Feel:** Clean, modern, innovative but approachable
- **Avoid:** The same SaaS template everyone else uses

### Education / Coaching
- **Colors:** Warm neutrals with one vibrant accent, or academic tones (forest green, burgundy)
- **Fonts:** Friendly and readable (Assistant, Rubik)
- **Feel:** Approachable, credible, motivating
- **Avoid:** Childish design for adult education

### Fitness / Sports
- **Colors:** High contrast --- darks with vibrant accents (orange, lime, red)
- **Fonts:** Bold, impactful Hebrew (Secular One, Karantina for display)
- **Feel:** Energetic, powerful, motivating
- **Avoid:** Overly aggressive "gym bro" aesthetic --- Israeli fitness is more balanced
